#whatifmusiced: Considering Possibilities, Crafting Praxis

Jesse RathgeberRead description

Venue: Room 303

Printed abstract credits: Jesse Rathgeber (ASU)

What ways of being musical are valued by the students with whom we work, the communities in which we work, and the larger society we inhabit? How might these values manifest in music learning spaces? What roles might technology and media play in empowering music learners and facilitators to engaged in culturally valued musicking. How might technology, media, and progressive education practices empower music learners and facilitators to co-construct new forms of musicking? In this participant-led session, we will discuss these and more. We will consider multiple and varied possible pathways ahead for music learning in contemporary music learning spaces by asking “what if.” With a focus on re-envisioning and reframing music education, we will explore possibilities and develop plans to turn our ideas into praxis. Come to share your ideas; come to explore new horizons of music education; and come to collaboratively plan ways ahead for music ed.

2015-07-30 · 11:15am EDT · Demo

FX Roulette

Adam Patrick BellRead description

Venue: Dolan Studio Live Room

Printed abstract credits: Adam Bell

FX Roulette provides participants with hands-on experiences in music making solely through the use of audio effects. It’s like the game hot potato, but with audio plugins (effects). Here’s how it works: Participants are given the “stems” of a song (the basic building blocks of a mix such as drums, guitars, bass, vocals, etc.) and are tasked with remixing it using only effects. The goal is to learn how audio effects work and experience how they can be used creatively. In each round of the game, the effects used are drawn at random. With each added round, less time is given, so by the end of the game participants must make faster decisions. Learners of all levels are welcome. No previous experience is needed. Please bring a laptop if you can (and I might install some free software on your computer if you need it).

Hip-Hop, Be-Bop, and Revolutionary Art: Collaborating to Bring Keith Haring and Jean-Michel Basquiat into Elementary Music and Art Education

Erica Quinn; Martina VasilRead description

Venue: 6th Floor Conference Space

Printed abstract credits: Martina Vasil, Erica Quinn

This session will guide participants through two hands-on projects for grades K–5 that infused music, art, and technology. Teachers from art and music classes collaborated to explore the public art of Keith Haring and Jean-Michel Basquiat. Students listened and moved to 1980s hip-hop and be-bop jazz music, examined and questioned artwork, built dance compositions, and created artwork that integrated similar symbols used by Haring and Basquiat. Additionally, students in grades 3–5 explored DJ music through a stories, videos, and a DJ application on tablet software. Students learned how to mix and record their own music using the DJ app. Participants in this workshop will be lead through the creative processes that students experienced. Critical questions about popular culture, technology, and education will be addressed.

Making Music with E-Textiles

Ryan BledsoeRead description

Venue: Dolan Studio Live Room

Printed abstract credits: Ryan Bledsoe

How do people, children included, want to interact with music? The design of any acoustic instrument is unavoidably linked to the sound it produces. Electronic instruments and computer-generated sounds are not attached to physical design in the same way. With devices such as microcontrollers, there are many ways for music makers of all ages to design controllers and sound-generating devices around their individual preferences. Certain microcontrollers are made for e-textile projects—electronic textiles or fabrics, also called wearables—where circuits are connected with conductive thread instead of wire. Designing instruments and controllers with e-textiles opens many educational and musical possibilities related to STEAM. To create a musical e-textile, one engages with design, prototyping, calculating, circuitry, programming the microcontroller, and aesthetics. Through this interactive experience, participants will engage with making music e-textiles and discuss the educational and musical implications.

2015-08-01 · 11:15am EDT · Demo

Teach Hip Hop’s Roots: Turntable Pedagogy in Game Format

Tom MaloneRead description

Venue: Dolan Studio Live Room

Printed abstract heading: Teach Hip Hop’s Roots - Turntable Pedagogy in Game Format

Printed abstract credits: Tom Malone

The project in development, “Catch a Groove,” is a culturally informed and carefully curated turntable pedagogy in the form of a ‘game’ using a cheap widely-available affordable digital DJ controller. Ideally suited to a Middle or High School general music classroom, this interactive curriculum has been heavily researched and field- tested with a sequential series of mixing lessons – presented to the learner as “levels” that must be mastered in order to proceed to the next. While most DJ pedagogy uses 1990s terminology with an emphasis on virtuosity and scratching, this game teaches the 1970s approach utilizing Grandmaster Flash’s “Breakmix” as the central organizing principle – which opens the door to a wider range of genres and artists and creative possibilities. Students not only learn the live reatime manipulation of existing tracks into loops and breaks, but also the cultural roots of the various funk, soul and disco records that formed the bedrock of Hip-Hop’s enduring rhythmic and stylistic vocabulary.
