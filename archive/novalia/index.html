MusEDLab / Collaboration archive

Kate Stone: Touch & Interactive Paper

Printed surfaces become musical interfaces through Novalia technology and MusEDLab design.

Kate Stone’s Novalia work connects physical print with sound and digital interaction. Novalia’s public description of PrintedTouch identifies conductive-ink sensors, microcontrollers and sound or Bluetooth modules as the building blocks of its interactive surfaces.

MusEDLab’s collaboration includes Paper MIDI designs by Diana Castro. NYU Integrated Design & Media’s published profile explicitly credits her Paper MIDI work with MusEDLab and Novalia UK.

This archive records the designs and their public presentation history. The images are documentation; they do not function as musical controllers in the browser.

Designing for touch

These two design records show different visual approaches to locating touch areas on paper: a keyboard layout and a branching composition of colored pathways. The source collection preserves multiple versions; the dates and filenames identify records rather than proving which version was manufactured.

Public presentation history

  • 2015 · IMPACT — Kate Stone’s keynote, “Paper MIDI Interfaces and Conductive Musical Stickers,” and the “Playing and Creating with Paper MIDI” session appear in the program.
  • 2016 · Interactive Surfaces Hackathon — MusEDLab’s public event announcement connected Novalia with Zachary Rotholz’s Chairigami cardboard furniture.
  • 2017 · MusED Hackathon — Kate Stone and Sunny Choi presented “Puzzlegram: Collaborative Music Making with Conductive Ink” in the August 5 program.
