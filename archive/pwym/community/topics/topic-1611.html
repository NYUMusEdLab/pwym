Reverse Engineering - In Your Eyes
[external link withheld] [link withheld]

Image omitted from this public edition
In Your Eyes.Convergent.png1280x704 188 KB
[link withheld]

The process - Listened to the given tracks while sketching a perceived space graph on paper. Next, I chose to work with the percussion first because the triangle really stood out as left and forward. I moved drums and bass in relation to percussion based on how they were placed on the space graph. Then, I work with the lead vocal by listening to the given track, then muting the the given and soloing the lead vocal. Next, I work on the piano and pads by panning them in relation to the original graph sketch. I mute and solo back and forth between the given track and the track to reverse engineer until they seem to match. I do the same procedure for the guitar and backing vocals. Finally, I mute and solo back and forth between my reverse engineered tracks and the given tracks to listen for a match. I moved some volume and pan on some of the tracks to tweak.

Community replyParticipant 7692016-06-29#39

Task 1:
The first thing I did when creating my mix was to isolate the Shak because that has a solo in the beginning and I found it easy to place the fader. After that, I worked on each component as it came into the piece, often referencing the original musical structure graph to help listen for the next part. I found it easy to place the strongest components of the piece, for example the lead vocals, Shak, and horns. I did find it challenging to place the beat holders, like the bass and drums, as they were in the ‘back’ of the sound. It was also difficult to find the accurate placement of the fade as the song became more and more layered with timbres.
First off, I learned how hard it can be to reengineer a piece of music. But, I truly learned how to train my ears to listen for each part of the music and how it comes into the ears. This exercise was much different from the perceived space graph and the musical structure graph. Although those projects helped me hear when a song part was or came in, this project made me create what I heard. This made my analyzation of songs more fine-tuned. I do believe I came very close to the reference mix. But, I don’t have the very best headphones so some of my placement in left versus right may be off.

Task 2:
Before beginning my mix, I listened to the original piece and detected my favorite parts and gave them more emphasis. As a fan of rock music, I wanted to emphasis the vocals, guitar, and bass as an attempt to change the genre to a more rock sound. While increasing the fade of the guitar, I reduced the horn portion. Another dynamic I changed was the prophet versus the horns. By increasing the synthesized sound of the prophet and reducing the horns, the sound become more modern.
A challenge I faced was how to change the sound of a song I had already heard. When I listen to a song, I never think about the components I would have liked to hear more or less of to match my musical taste, instead I would just decide if I liked the song or not. So to change the song entirely to tailor it to my ear was difficult at first. In addition, I found it hard to figure out what parts of the song changed the ‘vibe’ or genre sound. I learned that by turning up the guitar and vocals, the song became something that I would choose to listen to. Ultimately, I learned which parts of songs appeal to me, which now makes sense why I favor certain genres over others.
