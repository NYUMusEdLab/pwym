Anyone else here a bit of a fan of jazz style music? I don't particularly have in mind old-school, big band style jazz (though I like that too, and am happy to hang with people who are particularly into that), but more post-bop era music, particularly world-influenced fusion, but also straight up rock and pop-influenced fusion as well.

The foundational touchstone artists, in my mind, include the likes of Miles Davis, Herbie Hancock, Participant 718 Metheny, Jaco Pastorious, Thelonius Monk (pretty much my personal fave), John McLaughlin, Chick Corea, etc. Heck, even some Steely Participant 196 could fit in here (particularly their later stuff). Anything that takes its cue from jazz in terms of chordal originality and modal progressions, and fuses it with some high energy tempos, unusual time signatures or world rhythms.

EDIT - Other artists who have made use of jazz, fusion, and/or world influences, in no particular order, (let me know if there are others that should be added to this OP list). If it gets much longer, I might have to start creating sub categories, probably along the lines of those closest to true post-bop era stuff, smoother jazz, electronic influences, rock influences, world influences. Having said that, many of these people would easily fit into multiple categories:

For the Perceived Space Graph project, I picked The Nightfly, by Donald Fagen.

Embedded media omitted from this public edition

Not entirely sure how accurate this graph is, as the sound gets a little dense further in. I worked this graph from the first 1:50 of the song, and might get into the other sections of the song later.

[external link withheld] [link withheld]

The main spine of the song is the bass, drums, and the piano, which seem to remain fairly consistent in space all the way through. There's a slightly wet reverb on the snare, particularly, on the drums, but the kit mostly seems like it's all in the same space, as is designed to sound tight and relatively sparse.

The guitar part seems to move around quite a bit, with many of the licks seeming to be panned between left close and further away right (on a side note, although not included in this graph, Larry Carlton's solo seems mostly to be centre and far, behind the vox). The EP, also, while being fairly symmetrical, also seems to occasionally favour the right side, and seems often to move around in reply to the guitar licks. There are also various synth instruments used, often on the big almost percussive chords in the verses, but the main one I picked out sounds a bit like a theremin, which is usually far at the back favouring the left.

Vocals are pretty consistent, placed around the middle, with BVs behind that. A generous but short amount of reverb on these gives them a kinda spacey feel, which seems to work well against the tight and sparse rhythm section.

I picked this song firstly just because I like it, but because the way the sounds are arranged shows off the interesting chord changes and obviously the skill of the musicians, but also gets at the mood of the song, of a 'DJ with issues' manning the night shift - moody, groovy, and also a kind of subversion of the easy-listening late night jazz style.

Community replyParticipant 0392014-06-07#18

That's not a bad approach, tho - just imagine you're in a room, with a band jamming around and in front of you. Where does it sound like they're playing from? Are they jamming right in your ear, or are they off in a corner of the room? Try categorising instruments in terms of left and right, and near and far, in a list at first, if that's easier. When you have all the instruments you can pick out, then try and place them in 3 dimensional space.

There's no 'right' answer to this - I'm not at all convinced my graph is 100% accurate. The point, though, is that you start to think of recorded sound in terms of space as well as sound. And it is, in the end, just practice - I've been thinking of doing a couple more of these just to try and train my ear a bit more Image omitted from this public edition
