206Participant 2062014-05-19#2

Hi [participant mention] [link withheld] -- I'm more a process-oriented person myself, but that doesn't mean I don't envision, plan, and work towards creative goals. For me, I know I've always done my best when I'm very prepared, but I also believe, philosophically, that events only occur once. The mental image can be a part of that preparation, but after it's all done the outcome is a new entity, or a new manifestation, and I don't usually compare that original mental image to the outcome product. Even listening to a recording is a unique experience every time, though in theory the recording has stayed the same, I know I have changed. I'm very meticulous with music and I do usually like to develop abstract ideas about what I should be doing at any given moment, but when other things occur I leave my mind open to working with them instead of trying to force the preconceived mental image. In the end I believe music is multifaceted, you can view it from many points of view and in many different contexts, indeed it may be impossible to take in the totality of all its potential in any one incarnation. With recording, we'd all like to believe that the final published work is the best, most accurately executed version of a particular musical composition -- but I think in the back of our minds we all know that other, equally interesting, versions can exist through remix and rearrangement. So now it is but one point of view, probably no better or worse than a large variety of other versions.

206Participant 2062014-05-31#20

I don't usually wrestle, and so far as I know demons are not involved. In my view, what you're describing as "original inspiration," "concept image," "prized idea" etc, is part of the unseen world. I mean that it may be a perfectly formed and very concrete and real in your mind, but its manifestations in the outside, perceivable (by others) world, will always be somewhat incomplete. The full picture is only really a set of boundaries, one that you consciously create. Working with that idea in the seen world, you can operate within that set of boundaries, but it's impossible to fill in the entire picture at any single time. So maybe you have a tonality, or a set of chord changes, a bass line, or a drum beat. These things can expose the bounds of your imagination of this idea, but you can perform them in many different ways, and still have it be "the song".

Say you've written a song, you play it well and it fits in with your imagined idea of what it should be. Maybe you do some slight variations, play an instrumental section slightly different, emphasize a word in the vocals, or maybe change the groove a little bit or even rearrange the form. But essentially, you play the song the way you think it goes, the way it fits with your imagined ideas of what it should be even though those particular manifestations of the idea are different from each other.

Now say your song starts to get a little popular, and other people start playing it. They are not operating under the boundaries of your imagined idea, they're using their own template, even purposely distancing themselves from your original idea. Is it still the same song? Will it fit with your imagined idea? Will they surprise you and open up a whole other area of interest that you never even imagined, but that fits perfectly with your original concept? Maybe they'll do something you don't like at all, take it in a whole other direction, etc. Is it still the same song? You can do this exploration without other people being involved as well, if you allow yourself the freedom to explore.

If you accept my line of thinking here, you can see that the individual manifestations of a particular idea, no matter how well defined that idea may be in your mind, can all be different. So why worry about trying to perfectly match the idea, when there are so many possibilities to explore that you may not even know about? Get as close as you can, think about it critically and work on the aspects you know you can control, fix the technical glitches, find something that comes relatively close and don't worry about the rest. Perfectionism has its place, but always keep in mind that no-body and no-thing is perfect in the real world. That's my chime-in for the day.

005Participant 0052014-06-05#22

I learned to conquer my doubting demon by recognizing that pieces of music have a certain life of their own. They're memes that self-assemble in your head, or in the collective heads of your collaborators. You may have a certain amount of influence over their early gestation, but you can't control the way they come out any more than you can control the way your kids grow up. The best you can do is to be nurturing, to intervene when necessary, to stay out of the way when necessary, and to try to appreciate the final shape that the music takes on for itself. Imposing time limits on yourself is a big help. If you force yourself to stop working on something after a week, or a day, or an hour, you'll be pleasantly surprised by how beneficial the results are for your creations.
