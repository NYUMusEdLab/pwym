501Participant 5012014-05-23#24

Well...those definitely aren't the kind of problems I have had. If you do have someone that wants to back you, then you should take them up on it, as long as you keep creative and contract control. If you don't play well enough to put together arrangements then there are a lot of legitimate singers that use musical directors. A well known one of those, for example, would be Randy Jackson. This is usually a vocalist with chops along the lines of Barbra or Mariah or Celine. If you are not that kind of singer you're going to have to find a least one player(an arranger) that can help you get it studio ready even if you have to absorb that expense. When I was still teenager I was a steadily working demo singer in Nashville and it didn't really work for me except I realized I was going to have write cause there's no material for girls like me and GuitarTown wasn't going to be my homebase afterall cause I wasn't going to be a country and western singer. Generally, as an indie producer to make the business work I would expect 3 x's the budget back before I paid out %'s, if this is a situation where there is going to be a produced record for release. Boundaries are difficult to maintain with men in the entertainment business cause they are all out for it all the time. That's all I'm saying. That's the reason they get in the business.
